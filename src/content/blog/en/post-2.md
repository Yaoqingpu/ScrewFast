Thread mismatch is a common cause of delayed orders — and of leaks that surface months after a panel ships. Our factory can machine **NPT**, **G (BSPP)**, **ZG/R (BSPT)** and **metric** ends, but the RFQ must state which one you need: the two big families seal in completely different ways and are not interchangeable.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - NPT (ASME B1.20.1) is a 60° thread tapered 1:16 — ¾ inch per foot — that seals on its own flanks using PTFE tape or liquid sealant; G/BSPP (ISO 228-1) is a 55° parallel thread that never seals on the thread and instead seals on a bonded washer or O-ring at the face.
> - NPT and G are not interchangeable: some sizes share the same threads-per-inch, but the 60°-versus-55° flank mismatch galls the threads and the joint weeps at line pressure.
> - Sealant belongs on tapered threads only — dope on a G thread can keep the fitting from reaching proper torque and leave the face seal under-compressed.

## Two sealing philosophies

A tapered thread is its own seal. As an NPT male screws into an NPT female, the 1:16 taper wedges the flanks together, and the remaining spiral leak path is filled with sealant — PTFE tape or pipe compound. A parallel thread is only a fastener: G threads run in with clearance and spin home by hand, and the pressure-tight seal is made separately by crushing a bonded seal (a metal washer with a vulcanized elastomer ring), an O-ring in a recess, or a flat gasket against a machined face.

|                     | NPT                          | G (BSPP)                            |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Governing standard  | ASME B1.20.1                 | ISO 228-1 (ex-BS 2779)              |
| Thread form         | 60°                          | 55° Whitworth                       |
| Taper               | 1:16 (¾ in per ft)           | none — parallel                     |
| Seals on            | thread flanks + sealant      | face: bonded washer / O-ring        |
| Sealant needed      | yes                          | no — it can spoil the face seal     |
| Typical region      | North America                | Europe, Asia, export skids          |

## How to tell what you have

Read the marking first: "NPT", "G1/4", "Rp", "Rc" or "R" on the part or drawing settles it. With no marking, run calipers along the thread length — a male NPT's diameter visibly shrinks toward the end; a G male's does not. A pitch gauge helps, but similar TPI does not prove compatibility. A useful feel test: a G male spun into a matching G female turns home with clearance, while NPT starts easy and wedges within a few turns.

## Why mixing leaks

The traps look compatible and are not. NPT into G: the taper wedges against parallel threads until something yields, but there is no designed seal path — the joint feels tight and weeps under pressure. G into NPT: the parallel thread never wedges, and the face has nowhere to land. Even NPT into BSPT — both tapered at the same 1:16 — fails, because the 60° American flank and the 55° Whitworth flank ride on crest contact instead of the faces, galling on assembly and leaking under temperature or vibration. Cross-threaded "but it went in" joints are the most common thread callback we see on imported panels.

## The taper cousin: R / ZG (BSPT)

British taper pipe thread — R for the male, Rc for the female, ZG on many Chinese drawings — follows ISO 7-1 (older references cite BS 21 or EN 10226-1). It seals on the thread with sealant like NPT, and a G male can legitimately assemble into an Rc female where the design intends a thread-and-face combination — but R and NPT still do not mix. If a drawing says only "BSP," ask which variant before it becomes a purchase order.

## Regional conventions worth knowing

North American panels, compressors and utility skids run NPT almost exclusively. Europe, the UK, Australia, India, the Middle East and most Asian OEM equipment run G or R, with metric instrumentation ports (M5–M24 style) layered on top. Export skids we build routinely mix all three — a metric compression body on one end and an NPT or G male on the other — which is normal, provided the RFQ names each end explicitly.

## FAQ

**Can I seal NPT with a gasket instead of tape?**
No. NPT has no sealing face — the seal is thread flank interference plus sealant. A gasket on an NPT joint sits unloaded and does nothing; skipping the sealant leaves the spiral leak path open.

**Is G thread the same as BSP?**
Almost. BSPP — the parallel variant — is G, governed by ISO 228-1. BSP also includes BSPT, the tapered variant (R/Rc per ISO 7-1), which seals on the thread like NPT but with a 55° form. "BSP" alone on a drawing deserves a clarifying question.

**Why did my G-thread joint leak when I used plenty of PTFE tape?**
Because the seal is at the face, not the thread. Tape or compound on G threads lubricates the assembly, the fitting reaches a false torque before the bonded washer compresses, and the face seal stays loose. Use a bonded seal or O-ring, clean and dry.

**Are NPT and BSPT interchangeable if the TPI matches?**
No. The 1:16 tapers match numerically, but the 60°-versus-55° flank angle makes the threads ride on their crests, gall on make-up, and leak under load or vibration. Convert with a proper adapter — never force the pair together.

**How do I convert between NPT and G on an existing panel?**
With a male-female adapter machined for exactly the two standards involved, such as our [male-female straight adapters](/products/male-female-straight-adapter/) made to order. Adapters are cheap; cross-threaded valves and remade panels are not.
