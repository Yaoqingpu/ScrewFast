A "1.5 inch" **tri-clamp fitting** measures almost 2 inches across its flange — because tri-clamp sizes are named by **tube outside diameter**, not by the face the clamp actually grips. That single fact causes most ordering mistakes on sanitary lines.

## What a tri-clamp joint is

Three parts make the connection: two identical **ferrules** (a short tube stub with a raised flange), a **gasket** that sits between the ferrule faces in the bore, and a **clamp** that pulls the flanges together around it. No threads, no weld at the joint — which is why food, beverage, dairy and bioprocess lines use it: it breaks down for cleaning in seconds and leaves no crevices when made up.

## The size chart

| Nominal tri-clamp size | Tube OD | Ferrule flange OD |
| ---------------------- | ------- | ----------------- |
| 1"                     | 25.4 mm | 50.5 mm           |
| 1.5"                   | 38.1 mm | 50.5 mm           |
| 2"                     | 50.8 mm | 64.0 mm           |
| 3"                     | 76.2 mm | 91.0 mm           |

Note the trap: **1-inch and 1.5-inch fittings share the same 50.5 mm flange**. Flange OD alone cannot tell them apart.

## How to measure an existing fitting

1. Set calipers across the **outside edge of the ferrule flange** — the raised rim the clamp captures. Do not measure tube OD, flange thickness or the gasket.
2. Compare with the chart: about 64 mm flange means 2-inch; about 91 mm means 3-inch; 50.5 mm means 1-inch **or** 1.5-inch.
3. To split that last pair, measure the tube OD (25.4 mm vs 38.1 mm) or the bore, and confirm the standard on the fitting.

## Gaskets and clamps

Select the gasket for the **flange size**, plus material and service: Buna-N and EPDM for water and mild product, PTFE and Viton for aggressive media and temperature. Remember that a matching flange OD does not prove matching bores — when mixing inch and metric/DIN parts, verify both sides' dimensions and standards before assembly.

## Pressure rating

There is no single tri-clamp pressure rating. Working pressure depends on the clamp design, gasket material and condition, ferrules, temperature and assembly quality. Take the rating from the clamp and fitting maker for your actual conditions, and never exceed the lowest-rated component in the stack.

## FAQ

**Is tri-clamp the same as tri-clover?**
Tri-clover was the original brand name; tri-clamp became the generic term. Dimensions follow the same sanitary standards either way.

**How do I tell a 1-inch from a 1.5-inch tri-clamp?**
Both share a 50.5 mm flange. Measure the tube OD — 25.4 mm is 1-inch, 38.1 mm is 1.5-inch.

**What size clamp and gasket do I need?**
Match both to the flange OD (50.5 / 64 / 91 mm), and choose gasket material for your media and temperature.

**Are tri-clamp fittings high-pressure rated?**
They are sanitary connections, not high-pressure joints. Rating depends on the whole stack — clamp, gasket, ferrules — so confirm with the maker for your conditions.
