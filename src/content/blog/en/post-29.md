**KF vacuum fittings** — also called NW or ISO-KF — are the quick-flange standard for vacuum lines up to DN50, and their sizing is one of the friendlier systems in piping: the number after KF is the nominal bore in millimeters. A KF25 flange is built around a 25 mm bore. Everything else about the joint follows from how the flange seals.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - KF (Klein Flansche, ISO 2861 / DIN 28403) is a sexless quick flange: two identical flanges, a centering ring that both aligns them and carries an O-ring, and a single claw clamp — no bolts, no grooves, assembly in seconds.
> - Standard sizes KF16/25/40/50 name the nominal bore in millimeters, with flange outside diameters of 30 / 40 / 55 / 75 mm respectively; the system reaches roughly 10⁻⁷–10⁻⁸ mbar with elastomer seals, after which ISO-K/ISO-F (larger lines) and CF metal gaskets (ultra-high vacuum) take over.
> - The O-ring material sets the service envelope — FKM (Viton) is the common default for the 0–180 °C range — while the flange body in SS304/316 suits high vacuum and aluminum versions save cost on rough-vacuum plumbing.

## How a KF joint works

Two flanges, both identical — "sexless" in the standard's language — butt against each other with no groove cut into either face. Between them sits a centering ring: a metal ring whose bore aligns the flanges and whose outer channel holds an elastomer O-ring, the actual vacuum seal. A hinged claw or single-pin clamp wraps the flanged periphery and pulls the stack together. Assembly is seconds: seat the ring, swing the clamp, done. Disassembly is equally fast, which is why KF owns vacuum lines that are rebuilt as often as they are run — pump connections, forelines, chambers, gauges and the flexible jumps between them. ## The KF size chart

| KF size | Nominal bore (tube ID) | Flange OD | Typical use |
| ------- | ---------------------- | --------- | ----------- |
| KF10    | 10 mm                  | 30 mm     | Gauge ports, small forelines (shares clamp with KF16) |
| KF16    | 16 mm                  | 30 mm     | Instrument connections, small pump ports |
| KF25    | 25 mm                  | 40 mm     | The general workhorse — pump forelines, chamber ports |
| KF40    | 40 mm                  | 55 mm     | Larger forelines, process chambers |
| KF50    | 50 mm                  | 75 mm     | Biggest KF size — high-flow roughing lines |

Less common sizes KF20 and KF32 exist; above KF50 the standard steps to ISO-K/ISO-F. Note that the KF number is the bore, not the flange diameter — the clamp and centering ring must match the flange OD, which the chart fixes for each size.

## Materials and vacuum range

Flange bodies run in SS304 or SS316 for corrosion resistance and lower outgassing, with aluminum fittings popular for cost on rough-vacuum plumbing where the pressure rating note favors stainless for the deeper decades. With elastomer O-rings the system serves rough and high vacuum down to roughly 10⁻⁷–10⁻⁸ mbar depending on construction — beyond that, ultra-high vacuum needs metal seals: CF flanges with copper gaskets on small lines. The O-ring material is the service envelope: FKM (Viton) is the common default, rated about 0 to 180 °C in operation with short excursions higher; choose the elastomer for your bakeout and media, because an O-ring baked past its rating outgasses and leaks precisely where the pump works hardest.

## When KF ends and the bigger standards begin

KF covers lines up to DN50. Above that, ISO-K and ISO-F flanges take over — DN63 through DN630, joined by claw clamps (ISO-K) or bolted hole patterns (ISO-F) — for large process chambers and long vacuum lines. For UHV on small bores, CF flanges with metal gaskets replace KF entirely. The selection logic is a ladder: KF for quick assembly to 10⁻⁸, ISO for size, CF for ultimate vacuum. ## FAQ

**What does KF stand for, and is it the same as NW?**
KF is from the German Klein Flansche — "small flange." NW (Normen Weite, nominal width) is the same ISO 2861 / DIN 28403 system under its European name; KF and NW fittings of the same number are dimensionally the same system.

**What do the KF numbers mean?**
The nominal bore in millimeters — the largest tube inside diameter the flange is built around. KF16 ≈ 16 mm bore, KF25 ≈ 25 mm, and so on. The flange outside diameter is separate: 30 / 40 / 55 / 75 mm for KF16/25/40/50.

**How deep a vacuum can KF fittings hold?**
With elastomer O-rings, KF serves rough and high vacuum to roughly 10⁻⁷–10⁻⁸ mbar depending on construction and materials. Ultra-high vacuum beyond that needs metal-sealed CF flanges; large lines need ISO-K/ISO-F.

**KF16 and KF10 use the same clamp?**
Yes — KF10 and KF16 share the same 30 mm flange OD and the same clamp and centering-ring family, differing only in bore. Check the ring bore when ordering.

**When do I move from KF to ISO or CF?**
KF to DN50 and quick assembly; ISO-K/ISO-F for DN63 and above; CF metal gaskets for UHV on small lines. If the line is large, permanent, or pumping below 10⁻⁸, KF is no longer the answer.
