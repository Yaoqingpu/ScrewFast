Sometimes — but "stainless fitting" alone is not a compatibility guarantee. Whether a stainless **compression fitting** holds on copper or plastic tube depends on three things: ferrule material, tube hardness, and usually a **support insert**.

## Copper tubing

The conservative rule from major fitting makers: match the ferrule material to the tube. On copper, that generally means **brass ferrules and bodies**, not stainless — a hard stainless ferrule can bite or oval soft copper instead of seating cleanly. Some manufacturers flatly exclude stainless ferrules on copper tube in their installation specs. If you must join stainless to copper, use a fitting series the maker explicitly approves for that tube, and check the copper's hardness and wall first.

## Plastic tubing: nylon, PEX, PTFE

Several metal compression-fitting families are approved for plastics such as nylon, polyethylene, polypropylene, PTFE and vinyl — with normal makeup procedure **and a properly sized insert when required**. The insert supports the tube ID so the ferrule cannot collapse or creep it. Select the insert by both tubing OD and ID; a soft-tubing insert is not automatically right for rigid PEX or PTFE.

## The ferrule hardness rule

A ferrule must be harder than the tube it grips, but not so hard that it damages it. Too soft and the joint pulls out; too hard (stainless on soft plastic) and the ferrule scores or crushes the tube. For plastics, use only a fitting-and-ferrule arrangement the manufacturer expressly approves for that tubing.

## Pressure and temperature

There is no single rating for "stainless on plastic." The working limit belongs to the **complete assembly** — fitting, ferrule, insert, tube, fluid and temperature together. A 6000 psi stainless body tells you nothing about a nylon tube run at 60 °C. Get the assembly rating from the maker for your actual conditions.

## Common failure modes

- Leaks from under-tightening or skipping the prescribed turns
- Tube pull-out from inadequate grip or a missing insert
- Plastic creep under the ferrule over time
- Tube collapse or restriction without the required support
- Scored tube from a mismatched (too hard) ferrule

## FAQ

**Can I put a stainless compression fitting on soft copper tube?**
Most manufacturers recommend brass ferrules on copper. Stainless ferrules risk scoring or ovaling the tube. Use an approved series if you must mix.

**Do I always need an insert with plastic tubing?**
Use one whenever the fitting manufacturer requires it — typically on soft tubing. Choose it by tubing OD and ID.

**Why did my plastic tube fitting leak after a month?**
The usual causes are creep under the ferrule, a missing or wrong-size insert, or temperature above the assembly rating. Re-cut the tube past the grip marks and remake with a new ferrule set.

**Is PTFE tube treated like nylon?**
No. PTFE is rigid and needs its own approved fitting arrangement; do not assume a soft-tubing insert fits it.
