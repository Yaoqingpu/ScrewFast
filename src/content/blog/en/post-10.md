A compression joint can only fail two ways: at the seal or at the grip. **Single-ferrule** and **double-ferrule** fittings simply divide that work differently, and that difference decides which one belongs on your line.

## How a single-ferrule fitting works

One ring does both jobs. As you tighten the nut, the single ferrule bites into the tube OD and seals against the body nose in the same stroke. The stack-up is simple — nut, ferrule, body — with fewer parts and a lower price. DIN 2353 bite-type designs and progressive-ring (EO-style) fittings are the common single-ferrule patterns, and they serve general hydraulic and process lines well at their rated pressures.

## How a double-ferrule fitting works

Two rings split the load. The **front ferrule** forms the seal against the body and tube surface; the **back ferrule** grips the tube, resists pull-out and absorbs vibration. You get more parts and a stricter assembly order — but a joint that holds up better on vibrating instrument panels, gas service and high-pressure lines that get remade during maintenance.

## Side by side

| Factor                 | Single ferrule                       | Double ferrule                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Seal / grip            | One ring does both                   | Front seals, back grips              |
| Parts count            | Fewer                                | More, strict order                   |
| Vibration service      | Moderate                             | Better                               |
| Common design families | DIN 2353 bite type, progressive ring | Twin-ferrule instrumentation pattern |
| Cost                   | Lower                                | Higher                               |

## How to choose

- **Stationary hydraulic line, cost-sensitive:** a single-ferrule fitting rated for your pressure class is enough.
- **Instrument panel, gas service, vibration, or joints you will remake:** choose double ferrule.

Match the ferrule to the tube: the ferrule must be able to bite the tube, so fully annealed stainless tube within the maker's hardness range seats most reliably. Hard tube refuses a proper bite and leaks on gas service no matter which design you pick. ## FAQ

**Is double ferrule always better than single?**
No. For a stationary line inside the fitting's rating, single ferrule performs the same at lower cost. The double-ferrule advantage shows up under vibration, on gas service, and where joints are remade.

**Can I mix ferrules from different brands in one fitting?**
Do not. Front ferrule, back ferrule, nut and body are matched as a set by geometry and hardness. Mixing brands produces leaks at best and pulled joints at worst.

**What tube hardness works with double-ferrule fittings?**
Use fully annealed tubing in the hardness range the fitting maker specifies. Hard hydraulic tube is a common cause of first-time leaks on gas lines.

**Are single-ferrule fittings approved for gas service?**
Only when the specific fitting series carries that approval. Check the manufacturer's rating for the complete assembly — do not assume from the body material.
