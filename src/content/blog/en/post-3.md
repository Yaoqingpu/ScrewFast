## Why a factory beats a trading desk on lead time

A trader forwards your inquiry, waits for a factory reply, adds a margin, and forwards it back. Each loop eats days, and ambiguous specs bounce twice. A factory answers capacity, stock and price questions in one pass: the person quoting can see the barstock rack, the machining schedule and the packing bench. When the reply says a SKU is stocked, it ships; when it is not, the production window in the quote is real, not a placeholder. For export buyers planning installation crews and container schedules, that certainty is worth as much as the days themselves.

## Stocked sizes vs custom machining

Two different clocks run in any fittings factory, and the RFQ should say which one applies to each line item:

| Aspect        | Stocked SKUs                                                                                                          | Custom / non-stock                                                                                                          |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What it covers | Common compression fittings, ball valve bodies and hose ends in standard metric and imperial sizes, finished or semi-finished in the warehouse | Odd tube OD, special thread combinations, unusual material or altered geometry — barstock machining and inspection          |
| Lead-time clock | After PO confirmation, many of these SKUs leave within days                                                          | A clear production window, quoted up front so you can plan around it instead of discovering it after the deposit            |
| RFQ handling  | List as stocked lines and they can dispatch immediately                                                                | List as custom lines and the production window comes with the RFQ reply                                                     |

Splitting your BOM into "stocked" and "custom" lines at inquiry stage is the single best planning move — it lets you pull delivery forward with a partial shipment instead of waiting for the slowest line.

## How RFQ completeness changes quote speed

Every back-and-forth costs a day or more across time zones. A complete RFQ locks price and ship date in one pass:

1. **Size list or BOM** — one line per item: fitting type, tube OD or pipe size, thread on each end
2. **Material** — SS304 / SS316 / SS316L, per line if it varies
3. **Quantity** — per line; factories price by quantity breaks
4. **Destination country** — it drives packing, documentation and shipping method
5. **Certificates and documentation** — mill test certificates, third-party inspection or extra photo requirements, stated up front

Incomplete RFQs slow everyone down; complete ones get a firm answer quickly. If two families could fit your line, say so — a factory will propose the stocked series that ships fastest rather than quoting a specialty item by default.

## Export packing and documentation

Export fittings travel far and get handled hard, so packing is part of the product. Cartons are sized and cushioned for the fitting type, cap- and plug-protected where threads are machined, and consolidated for the container. A **packing list** travels with each shipment so customs and your warehouse can reconcile contents against the PO; we provide packing lists and packing photos when requested at inquiry, not after dispatch. Material certificates, when ordered, reference the heat numbers of the actual barstock used. If your country or customer requires specific documents — certificates of origin, extra inspection reports — name them in the RFQ so they are built into the schedule, not bolted on.

## MOQ and certificate reality

Minimum order quantities exist because machine setups have fixed costs: one 25-piece run of a custom fitting consumes nearly the same setup time as a 2,500-piece run. Small orders of stocked sizes are rarely a problem; small orders of custom geometries carry the setup burden, which is why consolidating custom items into fewer, larger lines saves real money. Mill certificates and third-party inspection add cost and a handling step — worth it for project and spec work, unnecessary for standard utility stock. Tell us which lines genuinely need paperwork; blanket certification across a whole BOM is money spent on documents nobody reads.

## FAQ

**How fast is "fast" for a stocked SKU?**
Many metric and imperial stocked sizes leave within days of PO confirmation — the binding constraint is usually payment and booking, not production. Custom or non-stock items get an explicit production window quoted with the RFQ reply, so both clocks are known before you commit.

**Can I split a shipment between stocked and custom lines?**
Yes — and you should. Shipping stocked lines immediately while custom lines run through machining gets fittings to your crew weeks earlier than holding everything for one consolidated dispatch. Ask for split-shipment pricing at quote stage.

**What documents come with an export shipment?**
A commercial invoice and packing list travel with every shipment. Material certificates, certificates of origin, third-party inspection reports and pre-shipment photos are available when requested — name them in the RFQ so they are scheduled, not scrambled for afterwards.

**Why does my custom fitting have a high minimum quantity?**
Machine setup — chucking, first-article inspection, program proving — costs the same whether the run makes 25 pieces or 2,500. Low-quantity custom orders pay that setup disproportionately. Consolidating custom geometries or accepting stocked equivalents where possible brings MOQ-driven pricing back down.

**Does a factory price beat a trader's price?**
Usually on identical spec, because there is one margin instead of two — but the bigger advantage is answer quality: stock status and production windows from a factory are firsthand facts, not relayed estimates. Compare quotes on total landed cost and stated lead time, not unit price alone.
